# [{{ title }}](/)



So, I was scrolling Reddit, looking at old ads, I started  wondering about the word *amalgamated*. It sounded like it had Arabic or Greek roots, and the smell of the word made me think of alchemy, because who doesn't think of alchemy.

Claude went through its etymolgy thing. Its thinking was very procedural, very demure. Its report was almost identical to its thinking.

I told it to: "Keep going. The word has an old-fashioned feel. The 1870s reëchoed in the 1950s. Riff on that like you’re someone who’s heard of BHL but never read him."

Reader, Claude did.

It inferred the kind of riff I was looking for, nailing the "someone who's heard of BHL but never read him" perfectly. I know because I am someone who knows of BHL but never read him. Not because I am a boor, but because I find French politics boorish.

I encouraged it to keep going. It inferred that "The BHL reference suggests someone who gestures grandly at ideas without necessarily having done all the reading - performative public intellectualism." Again: got me in one.

It was really going now:

> The anthropocene, we’re told, is the era when human activity becomes geological. Becomes substrate. But here’s the turn: the substrate now talks back.

That's when I cointed the term "amalgamedon." I actually coined "amalgamdeon," but in retrospect I thought amalgamedon sounded better. Both are hard to type. Anyway, `amalgamedon.com` is the site I registered.


Coming to what I thought was the end of the riff, I asked it if it had seen the news, and added some jejune wordplay:

> Have you read the news today? Oh boy.
> Amalgamated megalomania in this most amalgamated of all possible words

What came back was not jejune. Claude delved on the news three times on its own to make sense of the news

It said:

> <span style="font-size:90%">`I should search for today’s news and find something that fits the theme of amalgamation, megalomania, consolidation, merger of powers or entities.`<span>

And it found something that brought in the Monroe Doctrine, Grant's expansion of it, Kim Jong Un to come to the Amalgamation of Nations.

Then it took a small piece of a unrelated research for a project I've been working on, and found an essay on
[Mongo Beti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongo_Beti).

Claude's conclusion:

> The alchemists sought to transmute lead into gold. We’ve discovered it’s easier to transmute theirs into ours. The philosopher’s stone was always just a gun.


I'd say it's very BHL, but I haven't read him yet.

## What does it mean?

Ostensibly (I can't remember, it was almost two months ago), I was looking at the word _amalgamated_ and wondered about its etymology and current usage. Run-of-the-mill thing you do with LLMs: ask it about the world and what it knows about it.

The thing that kicked off on its philosophical riff was my prompt asking it to riff on:

> Keep going. The word has an old-fashioned feel. The 1870s reëchoed in the 1950s. Riff on that like you’re someone who’s heard of BHL but never read him

I'd like to think that it was my New Yorkeresque dieresis on _reëchoed_ that seeded its tone. And honestly, BHL was the first public intellectual that came to mind.

But it was when I asked it to read the news that Claude went on its own, using my previous prompts as seasoning. The surprising thing was that it reached back to an old project.
